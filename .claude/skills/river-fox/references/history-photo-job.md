## 1. Look and ask (before spending anything)
1. List the folder; ignore duplicate "(1)" copies and `Duplicates` folders; never delete anything in Laura's folder. Note any video files and set them aside for the video skill.
2. Look at every photo (contact sheet, Read it). Note faces, lettering, night or dusk shots, the best-looking frames.
3. **Ask Matt, in one short message** (he answers on his phone):
   - **Where was it?** Town or area is enough for a private home; never a street or house number. (Worplesdon, Surrey.)
   - **Any words he wants in the caption or on the image**: quirky words, Laura's own line, a client's first name only if the client is happy to be named.
   - A bit more about the job so the copy is true: what it was (garland arch, wreath, balloon arch), the occasion, anything Laura said about it, whether it belongs to a campaign (Christmas 2026) or just a channel, and which platforms (default: all).
   - **If he gives no copy, suggest 3 hooks yourself** from the photos and the answers (see Copy below) and let him pick. Never invent the final wording alone: an invented line ("Christmas, at the door.") was queried on 20 Nov.
4. If a photo is a compressed copy (WhatsApp, Instagram), say so and ask for the phone original; carry on with the copy if he can't.

## 2. Grade
- Put the photos in `tools/photo-grade/state/inbox/YYYY-MM-DD-short-subject/`, each with a descriptive unique name that includes the place (`porch-garland-arch-wreath-front-mid-view`). Add a note in `photos.json` for each (what must not move: house, colours, lettering, sky). Faces: skip.
- **Night or dusk shots:** `"preset": "gentle"` and a note that it must stay a dusk photograph (keep the sky, the lights, the warm windows; lift shadows only gently). Expect "scene drifted"; look closely at the before | after.
- Dry run, then run: `npm run run -- --only=<job folder or a name shared by all photos>` (the job folder works now). About $0.08 a photo.
- Read every `state/compare/<name>.jpg` and check what the flags mean before showing anyone. One re-grade with `--force` per photo at most.

## 3. Export
Also make a **Large master** of every photo with `npm run large -- --only=<name shared by the job> --out=<website folder>/Large (1632 wide)`: the model's full-size result (1632 x 2160 for a tall photo; it can't go bigger, so say that) as WebP quality 82, next to the 400/700/1000/1400 set. Those are for heroes and the full-screen viewer, not the grids (about 700 KB each).
`npm run export -- --only=<job> --for=website,instagram,story,google-business,facebook`. Look at `sheet.jpg`: for tall photos check the Story crop (about 16% of the width is lost) and the Google Business crop. Warn Matt about WebP files over budget.

## 3b. Cull: pick the best, keep all edited originals (Matt, 21 Nov 2026)
Laura takes many near-identical photos, so exporting all of them for Instagram, Facebook and Google Business is too many. **After grading, look at every edited photo together (numbered contact sheet) and group near-duplicates (same angle, same subject).** Keep only the best of each group: prefer the wider shot when it shows the whole piece, or the closer shot when it shows the detail we want to feature, and never two almost-identical angles. Always keep a unique shot (a dusk photo, a distinct close-up). Say which you kept and why, in a short table.
- **Folder layout:** each platform folder (Instagram post, Instagram story and Reel, Facebook, Google Business) holds **only the picks**, laid out natively (the text overlay file stays, `00 - with text - ...`). **All edited photos stay in one folder, `_Originals (all edited)`, in the job folder** (underscore so it sorts to the top): the non-picks at each platform size in a subfolder per platform, plus `Large graded masters (1632 wide)`. Nothing is deleted.
- Worplesdon example (14 photos, 6 kept): left-angle-close-tall, left-angle-olive-ribbon-pine-cones, night-fairy-lights-dusk-front, right-angle-wreath-door, wreath-front-close-gold-baubles, wreath-front-mid-view.

## 4. Text on the best photo (not on every photo)
- Pick the **one best photo per format** (Instagram post 3:4, Story or Reel cover 9:16). Good means: the whole subject visible, calm space for the hook (gravel, sky, floor) rather than garland or faces, and a different photo for the Story when it gives the text more room. Say why you picked them. The hook never sits on the subject; if no spot is calm, use the middle band and call it out.
- Run `npm run overlay -- --job=<job> --hook1=... --hook2=... --caps=... --post=<name> --story=<name>`. It uses Fraunces Regular (400, Matt's decision 20 Nov: "Regular everywhere", the Light 300 was hard to read), a caps line, the handle on Stories only, and fits the text inside the safe area. Check the printed line: fonts loaded, text inside the safe area.
- Read both outputs at full size. Legible at grid size? Not crowding the door, the faces or the handle? Fix and re-render before showing.
- **Google Business gets no text, ever.** Plain photo only.

### Copy
Laura's voice: first person, warm, plain words, name the real thing, no exclamation marks, no em dashes, no banned praise words (stunning, amazing). Two short lines, about five words, first upright, second italic, then one line of capitals saying what it is, including the place when it helps ("A GARLAND ARCH AND WREATH IN WORPLESDON, SURREY"). Offer three options in a small table; hooks that use the place and the real thing ("A Worplesdon welcome.") beat generic ones. Matt's quirky words go in as given.

## Lessons log
- **20 Nov 2026, Worplesdon garland and wreath (13 photos + a night shot):**
  - Matt wants one overlay on the best photo per format, not on all of them.
  - Matt asked why the hook was "Christmas at the door": it was invented. Ask for the location and any wording first; suggest copy if none.
  - Fraunces Light 300 (pack v1.18) was "hard to read"; Regular 400 everywhere. He updated the pack to v1.19.
  - A busyness score can't tell gravel from garland; choose the text position by eye.
  - On a 9:16 crop of a close shot the arch fills the frame; the wider shot leaves room for text.
  - The hook must not wrap ("A Worplesdon" split onto three lines until the line was set not to wrap and the type shrank to fit).
  - `--only` didn't match inbox job folders (fixed in the tool).
  - A compressed WhatsApp copy of the night shot was graded; ask for the phone original.
  - Matt asked for large website images: the 400-1400 set matches the site's gallery, so also give the 1632 master. Folders: he found `Campaigns › Christmas › Images › ...` confusing; one dated folder per job directly in the campaign, and no separate "with text" folders.
  - **Asking Matt to choose (21 Nov 2026):** use a visual decision page (stills to tap), one numbered card per decision with a bold question and a one-line reason, checkboxes so he can pick several (or all), a "none yet" option, and a copyable summary of every pick. He liked the picture-tapping; sections needed to be clearer and he often wants several versions to compare.
  - **Tone of written copy (21 Nov 2026):** "Your door's sorted" was called lowbrow. No slang or casual phrasing in headlines, finales or overlays; calm, warm, considered. Finale for a garland job: "Christmas has arrived."
  - **Reel pacing (21 Nov 2026):** F (hook first) is Matt's favourite. After an opening close-up that zooms out, cut to Laura as soon as the zoom-out has finished (about 1.8s), not later.
  - **Filing videos (21 Nov 2026):** reels for a job go in the same job folder as its photos, in a `Video - Reels 9x16 (1080x1920)` subfolder, files named `YYYY-MM-DD - <place> - Reel X (...)` with the date of the job, the favourite and the cover prefixed `00 - `, the large masters in `_Masters (large, 1080x1920)`. Export masters at CRF 17, then a post copy capped at 10 Mbps (`-crf 23 -maxrate 10M -bufsize 20M`, about 35-50 MB for 35 s) for Instagram.
  - The same folder also held 24 video clips: those are a separate job (video skill).
