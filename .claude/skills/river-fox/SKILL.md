# River Fox (the one skill)

**Brand and social rules live in the Brand Pack canvas. Read the relevant board before building. Never copy rule values into this skill.** (Matt, 23 Nov 2026.) That means no sizes, pixel positions, fonts, weights, colours, safe areas or fades are written here: each step below names the board to read, and the tools take their values from one file, `tools/river-fox/brand-pack.json`, which names the board behind every value. When a board changes, change that file in the same session; if a tool and the canvas differ, show Matt both and ask. Voice and brand colours are the Brand book's, not the canvas's.

This front page holds the build steps: what to ask, which tool to run, how to cull, export, name and file. If it disagrees with a file in `references/`, this page wins; the references are older history and any look or size written in them is out of date.

## 0. Start of every job
1. Read the Brand Pack canvas first (the "River Fox Brand Pack" artifact, with the Artifact tool), not the pack PDF (Matt, 23 Nov 2026): it is the source of truth for sizes, fonts, safe areas, covers and review cards. Then the newest Brand book PDF in Drive (never `_Archive`) for voice and colours. Use the pack PDF only if the canvas can't be read, and say so. Say what you read.
2. Look at every photo and video (contact sheets). Ignore "(1)" duplicates; never delete anything in Laura's folder.
3. Ask Matt once, in one short message: where it was (town only, never an address), any words or quirky wording he wants, what the job was, campaign or everyday, platforms. If he gives no copy, suggest three options from the footage and let him pick. Never invent final wording alone. Ask before sending any audio to OpenAI (once per job).
4. For video, analyse the footage and suggest 3 to 5 hooks as stills (her best reaction, a title behind her head, a surprising detail, before and after). Open on the strongest moment; words about seven or fewer. **A time-lapse job always gets two separate storyboards, A build first (the time-lapse starts on frame one, the reveal after it) and B hook first (the strongest moment, then the build).** Label them A and B, say which I would post and why, and build only what he picks.
5. Stills first, never videos, until he picks. Decision pages: numbered cards, one question each, tick boxes so he can pick several, a copyable summary.
6. **Every video storyboard's decision page includes this question: "Do you want alternative versions in other caption fonts?"** Pink box captions is ticked by default; Wild font captions and Classic font captions are optional extras he can tick (one or both, or none). Show a still of each style on his own footage so he can see the difference. Build only what he ticks. Covers are not part of the question (they have their own board).
7. For a campaign, the decision page also asks: "Do you want a new Facebook page cover, with and without words?" (section 1), but only if the job has a landscape photo.

## 1. Photos
- Edit with `tools/photo-grade` (OpenAI image model, one fixed house look: bright and airy, true whites, open shadows). Put photos in `state/inbox/<job>/` with descriptive names that include the place; add a note per photo for lettering and colours that must not move. Faces are never graded. Dusk or night shots stay dusk (`gentle` preset and a note). About $0.08 a photo. Read every before and after yourself.
- **Cull:** group near-duplicate angles, keep the best of each (wider shot for the whole piece, closer for the detail), never two almost identical angles. Keep all edited photos in `_Originals (all edited)` in the job folder; the platform folders hold only the picks.
- Exports: `npm run export -- --only=<job> --for=website,instagram,story,google-business,facebook`; a large master for the website (`npm run large`, 1632 wide, say plainly it can't go bigger). Google Business photos have no text, ever.
- **Thumbnails and covers:** read the canvas boards "Covers and thumbnails" and "Light photos and long hooks" first. Make every cover with `tools/river-fox/text/thumb.cjs` (or `npm run overlay`, which hands the photo to it); never draw one any other way. It takes the photo, the size and the two hook lines, and prints what it did: the fade, whether it treated the hook as long (line 2 smaller) or short (one size), each line's size and width, and where the headline and handle sit. Read that print-out. It refuses a hook that does not fit; shorten the hook, don't override. `--hook=long` or `--hook=short` overrides its word count when a hook needs it; there is one fade and no option to change it. Offer a daylight and a dusk version. Before filing a cover, crop it to the middle 4:5 and the middle 3:4 and look at both, beside the last cover posted. Crop or choose photos so no client number plates, phone numbers or street addresses show.
- **Facebook page cover (the banner at the top of her page), an option on every campaign:** read the canvas board "Facebook page cover · rules and crops" for the size, the safe box, what the words may say and the one small-capitals exception. Steps: ask on the decision page "Do you want a new Facebook page cover for this campaign?"; only when the job has a landscape photo (if there is none, don't ask; say so and that Laura would need to take one); suggest three wordings in the board's shape and let him pick; make two files from the campaign's best wide photo, with words and plain; check the result on a phone and a computer. File in `Facebook - Page cover (1640x624)`: `01 - Page cover - With words.jpg`, `02 - Page cover - Plain.jpg`. Laura changes the cover herself; never touch her page.

## 2. Video
- **Caption style:** read the canvas boards "Video captions, three styles" and "How captions move". Pink box is the default reel; Wild font and Classic font are made only when asked. One version by default, not three. `tools/river-fox/captions/anim.cjs` draws all three from `brand-pack.json`.
- **Caption timing (build detail, not on the canvas):** each word fades in about 0.06s before she says it (fade about 0.13s). Find each word's onset from her voice at 20 ms steps, never from a transcript alone; each separate burst is a word. A held word's letters arrive one by one across the hold; never split a phrase across screens.
- Captions never cover her face: check every caption frame against where her face is.
- **Headline and closing text on video:** read the canvas board "Headline and closing text on video". Make them with `text/mkcopy2.cjs`, which prints where the lettering sits and will not write one outside the safe area. If the small capitals line under the closing text does not fit inside the safe area, remove it rather than shrink or move the headline. No casual or lowbrow wording.
- Text on video is narrower than a cover (the board gives the limit; `mkcopy2.cjs` fits to it). Never reuse a cover's width on video.
- **Safe areas:** the canvas board "Safe areas" has the numbers for Reels, Stories and covers; `mkcopy2.cjs` and `reels/check.py` read them from `brand-pack.json`. Use `STORY=1` / `--story` for a Story.
- **Audio** (canvas board "Sound and colour"): no music bed, not even looped room tone (it was heard as a repeating noise). Trim her speech to just around her words with a light gate.
- Timelapse: two storyboards, build first and hook first (section 0, step 4). Fix flicker and colour with `timelapse/stabilise.py`, crop centred on the arch with margins, no pan if the arch fills the width. The finale text only goes on the time-lapse if its last frame shows the finished job (`reel-E-timelapse-build.sh` shows that frame and waits for a yes); otherwise cut to Laura's own reveal and put the finale there.
- Cutting (build detail): hard cuts, never in the first 4 seconds, no animated zooms or shakes, no end card. End a talking shot with her eyes open, once her words have finished.
- **Run `reels/check.py` on every finished video, draft and final, and read its sheet before sending or filing:** frame 0, the frame either side of every cut, every text against the red safe lines, where sound starts and stops. A stray frame or text outside the lines fails. Say “inside” or “clean” only for what it measured.
- **The working folder is kept: `~/river-fox-work/<job>` on the Mac** (never a temporary folder, never Drive). The reel scripts save the picture with no text on it in its `plates/`. A re-cut is made from those, never from a finished video; if it has to be, cut by frame number and re-check, because it inherits that video's faults.
- Cut after an opening close-up as soon as it has finished (about 1.8s), not later.
- Wild card Story: an option, offered on talking clips where she moves (canvas board "Wild card Story"; `tools/river-fox/cutout/`).
- Exports: drafts are 540x960 on purpose. Final 1080x1920, CRF 18, max 25 Mbps (about 105 MB for 35 s), one file per option. A 4K file only when asked, and say it is upscaled unless the footage is real 4K. No separate "master" copies.

## 3. Words
- **Three voices, by channel (Matt, 21 Nov 2026, after reading all 324 of Laura's Instagram posts):**
  - **Website (and Google Ads): the brand-book voice.** Calm, warm, considered, first person, plain words, no exclamation marks, no emojis, none of the banned praise words.
  - **Google Business posts: a halfway house.** A little more playful than the website, not as full-on as her social feed: warm and friendly, first person, at most one exclamation mark, at most one emoji (or none), no drawn-out words, a clear ask and the page link.
  - **Instagram and Facebook captions: Laura's own social voice, playful.** In her last year (123 posts): 93% use emojis (about 2 to 3 a post), 72% use an exclamation mark (about 1 to 2), captions about 450 characters. Shape: a short playful hook with an emoji ("Today's doorway", "It's tiiiiime!!", "For my next trick...."), two or three warm sentences about what she made and why, a plain ask ("DM me a photo of your doorway together with your postcode"), often a note on how few dates are left, then a column of dots and the hashtags. Favourite words: love, little, beautiful, perfect, magic. **"Stunning" and "wow" are allowed here: they are her own words** (Matt, 21 Nov; the brand book's ban on them applies to the website). Mostly "I"; keep "I".
  - Facebook: her page (facebook.com/riverfoxeventssurrey; the address changed from the old lollipopballoonsx on 21 Nov 2026) carries the same captions as Instagram, cross-posted with the hashtags (43 recent posts checked, 21 Nov 2026). So one caption serves both; the Facebook version may add the website link.
  - **Always curly quote marks and apostrophes (’ and “ ”), never straight ones (' and ")** (Matt, 21 November 2026): in every piece of text set onto an image or video (review cards, headlines, covers, captions burned into video) and in the written captions and the Copy doc. Look for straight ones before filing. The review tool converts them itself and prints how many it changed.
  - Text designed onto images and video (headlines, covers, video text) stays in the calm brand-book voice on every channel: no slang ("sorted"). Never on any channel: "luxury" hashtags, florist or planner words, the old business name.
- **Hashtags** (the rule is on the canvas board "Captions and hashtags"; the tags are in the Hashtag bank sheet): build each post's set from the sheet's Active tab, add the town to the bank if it is new, never use anything on the Never use tab, and afterwards add one row per post to the sheet. "River Fox Events Surrey" is not a hashtag.
- **Copy doc** (the last step of every job): a Google Doc `Copy doc - <job>` at the top of the job folder with only these five sections, in this order, each written in its channel's voice (above): Instagram Reel, Instagram post, Facebook post (each caption ends with a column of five dots and its five hashtags; Facebook says "message me" instead of "DM me" and adds the page link), Google Business post (halfway voice, no hashtags, then "Button: Book" and the page link), then Google Ads (calm voice, Book now button, page link, 3 headlines of 30 characters or fewer, 2 descriptions of 90 or fewer). Nothing else. The Worplesdon Copy doc (21 Nov 2026) is the model to copy. A thin grey divider line sits between the sections (a top border on each heading after the first, `update_doc` `updateParagraphStyle` `borderTop`). Read Laura's recent captions first (open her Instagram in the Browser pane) and match how she writes.

## Review cards (Matt, 21 November 2026)
A client's review made into an image. This section is the whole rule for review cards; where it differs from the general steps (section 0's questions and decision pages, section 4's folders), this section wins.
- Make them with `tools/river-fox/review/review.cjs`: give it the size (`instagram` or `google`), the quote, the name and the output file. It takes every size and colour from `brand-pack.json`, picks the short or long setting from the quote's length, and prints what it used, each line's width and the space to every edge.
- Before each review job read the canvas boards "Review post · short and long rules" and "Google Business review · short and long rules" (use the pack PDF only if the canvas can't be read, section 0, step 1).
- Always the full review, in the client's own words, unless Matt says otherwise. Emojis are left off.
- **Spelling and grammar check every quotation before it goes on an image (Matt, 21 November 2026). This rule beats the Brand book: if the book says typos stay as the client wrote them, fix them anyway (Matt, same day).** Read the quote through and fix clear slips only: a misspelt or wrong word ("and beautiful" for "are beautiful"), a missing full stop or capital between sentences, a missing full stop at the end. Never reword, shorten, tidy her style or change her punctuation choices (her exclamation marks and "stunning" stay). Tell Matt every change made, old and new, when handing the cards over. The check includes a comma before "and" when it joins two full sentences ("are beautiful, and the natural colours suit..."). **Only this tool makes the review PNGs in Drive.** If a card in Drive came from anywhere else (the Claude chat re-exported Sarah's on 21 Nov and the full stops were lost), read it word by word and run this check before saying it is fine, then remake it here. This is for quotes designed onto images; the website shows reviews exactly as Google has them.
- Make both sizes every time (Instagram and Google Business). No caption.
- Check the result at phone size before filing: even lines, no single word on the last line, and a long quote must not have gone small.
- Filing: a review for a job that has its own folder goes in a `Review - <first name>` folder inside that job's folder, with older cuts in its `_Archive`. Otherwise file by channel in Social > Instagram > Review posts and Social > Google Business > Review posts.
- Come back with the finished cards filed and say where they are. Don't offer versions first.

## 4. Filing and names
- **Job and campaign folders: `NN - <location or event name>`** (e.g. `01 - Worplesdon garland and wreath`), 01 = newest; when adding a job, rename the others up by one (highest first). The type (Images or Video) is NOT in the name because one job holds both. **No Finder colour tags except one: the newest (01) gets the Purple Finder tag** (Finder has no pink; write it with `xattr -w -x com.apple.metadata:_kMDItemUserTags` and a binary plist of `Purple\\n3`, then `killall Finder`). When a new job becomes 01, remove the tag from the old one (`xattr -d`). It also has a pink Drive folder colour (`update_file` `folderColorRgb` `#f691b2`) for Drive on the web and phone; reset the old one to Drive's default grey (`#8f8f8f`). Archive folders are always `_Archive`.
- One folder per place it will be posted: `Instagram - Reels (9x16)`, `Instagram - Stories (9x16)`, `Instagram - Posts (3x4)`, `Facebook - Posts (4x5)` (with the same two covers first, made by `thumb.cjs` at the Facebook post size), `Google Business - Posts (4x3, no text)` (plain photos only, no covers, no text). Files are short and numbered and say the kind, not the place: `01 - Video - Pink box captions.mp4`, `05 - Cover - Dusk.jpg`, `03 - Photo - Front view.jpg`. Same photo names and order in every folder. Superseded files go to `_Archive`.
- Laura's originals stay where she uploaded them. Never overwrite; never leave scratch work in Drive.

## 5. Check before sending
`reels/check.py` run on every video and its sheet read; every cover's and text image's print-out read; text inside the safe area; captions off her face; every word matches her voice at that frame; each piece of copy is in its channel's voice (website and Ads calm with no exclamation marks or emojis, Google Business at most one of each, Instagram and Facebook playful); no em dashes anywhere; curly quote marks and apostrophes, no straight ones; review quotes spelling and grammar checked; five hashtags; Google Ads limits; every link returns 200; sound silent outside her voice; the folder and file names. Say what you checked.
