# River Fox (the one skill)

**Brand and social rules live in the Brand Pack canvas. Read its rules for what you are making before building. Never copy rule values or board names into this skill.** (Matt, 23 and 25 Nov 2026: the boards are merged and renamed often, so this skill says what to look up, never which board it is on.) That means no sizes, pixel positions, fonts, weights, colours, safe areas or fades are written here, and the tools take their values from one file, `tools/river-fox/brand-pack.json`, where each group says what it is about (covers, review cards, safe areas...). When the pack changes, change that file in the same session; if a tool and the canvas differ, show Matt both and ask. The canvas is also the brand book (the same thing); there is no brand PDF and no version of the pack is ever quoted. **If the canvas can't be read, stop and tell Matt; never fall back to anything else.** Voice rules are not on the canvas: they are in `docs/voice.md` in this repo, the only place they are written.

This front page holds the build steps: what to ask, which tool to run, how to cull, export, name and file. If it disagrees with a file in `references/`, this page wins; the references are older history and any look or size written in them is out of date.

## 0. Start of every job
1. Read the live Brand Pack canvas (the "River Fox Brand Pack" artifact, with the Artifact tool) for the rules this job needs (find them by subject: covers, review cards, captions, safe areas...), and `docs/voice.md` for the words. Compare those rules with `tools/river-fox/brand-pack.json`; if the canvas has changed, update that file (and any tool that can't follow it) before building, and say so. If the canvas can't be read, stop and tell Matt: there is no fallback. Say what you read.
2. Look at every photo and video (contact sheets). Ignore "(1)" duplicates; never delete anything in Laura's folder.
3. Ask Matt once, in one short message: where it was (town only, never an address), any words or quirky wording he wants, what the job was, campaign or everyday, platforms, **and whether to add it to the website's Recent work** (Matt, 2026-11-24: ask on every job with new photos). If he gives no copy, suggest three options from the footage and let him pick. Never invent final wording alone. Ask before sending any audio to OpenAI (once per job).
4. For video, analyse the footage and suggest 3 to 5 hooks as stills (her best reaction, a title behind her head, a surprising detail, before and after). Open on the strongest moment; words about seven or fewer. **A time-lapse job always gets two separate storyboards, A build first (the time-lapse starts on frame one, the reveal after it) and B hook first (the strongest moment, then the build).** Label them A and B, say which I would post and why, and build only what he picks.
5. Stills first, never videos, until he picks. Decision pages: numbered cards, one question each, tick boxes so he can pick several, a copyable summary.
6. **Every video storyboard's decision page includes this question: "Do you want alternative versions in other caption fonts?"** Pink box captions is ticked by default; Wild font captions and Classic font captions are optional extras he can tick (one or both, or none). Show a still of each style on his own footage so he can see the difference. Build only what he ticks. Covers are not part of the question (they have their own board).
7. For a campaign, the decision page also asks: "Do you want a new Facebook page cover, with and without words?" (section 1), but only if the job has a landscape photo.

## 1. Photos
- Edit with `tools/photo-grade` (OpenAI image model, one fixed house look: bright and airy, true whites, open shadows). Put photos in `state/inbox/<job>/` with descriptive names that include the place; add a note per photo for lettering and colours that must not move. Faces are never graded. Dusk or night shots stay dusk (`gentle` preset and a note). About $0.08 a photo. Read every before and after yourself.
- **Cull:** group near-duplicate angles, keep the best of each (wider shot for the whole piece, closer for the detail), never two almost identical angles. Keep all edited photos in `_Originals (all edited)` in the job folder; the platform folders hold only the picks.
- **Website Recent work (when he says yes at step 3):** the best photo goes into the Our work gallery and onto the top of the one latest-work list, `src/data/recentWork.ts` in this repo, whose header has the steps; it feeds every Recent celebrations wall on the site (home, town pages, /services/), which stay at three rows, so the oldest drops off. Then open a PR (never push to `main`).
- Exports: `npm run export -- --only=<job> --for=website,instagram,story,google-business,facebook`; a large master for the website (`npm run large`, 1632 wide, say plainly it can't go bigger). Google Business photos have no text, ever.
- **Thumbnails and covers:** read the pack's cover rules first. Make every cover with `tools/river-fox/text/thumb.cjs` (or `npm run overlay`, which hands the photo to it); never draw one any other way. It takes the photo, the size, the headline (one or two lines) and, when the headline needs more words, one `--caps` line, and prints what it did: the fade, the word count, the headline size (both lines the same size, sized to the words), the capitals line, where everything sits, and how busy the photo is under the words compared with the other spots. Read that print-out. **Choose the spot by looking at the photo: words never sit on the subject if there is a calm spot** (`--at=middle`, the default, `--at=top` or `--at=<y>`); when the print-out says a calmer spot exists, look again. It refuses a headline that would come out too small (shorten it or move words to `--caps`), a one-word headline without its full stop, and words too close to the handle or an edge; don't override. `--tint` adds the pack's even dark tint when the words still don't read; `--no-handle` is for a very busy photo. It looks for faces itself (Apple Vision): it refuses words on a face (move them under the face with `--at=<y>`, or use colour blocks on a Reel cover) and leaves the handle off when its fixed spot would land on one. **Second style, colour blocks** (the pack's cover rules; Matt 25 Nov 2026): when the words would land on a face or the subject, use `thumb.cjs ... --blocks` (pink blocks, one per line). With a face (Laura close up), the tool places them itself, never on the eyes, nose or mouth: above the eyes if there is room below the pack's top line, else below the chin, else over the forehead, stepping the size down if it must (Matt, 25 Nov 2026). Without a face, the tool won't guess: choose the spot by eye where the photo is calm, not one fixed height every time (`--at=top`, `middle`, `lower`, a y, or `--between=<y0>,<y1>`). It prints how busy the photo is under the blocks and in the other spots, and says when a clearly calmer one exists: look before accepting it. Reel covers only. The photo is never moved or extended. It refuses a block that runs into the Reel icon in the grid, and leaves the handle off when its fixed spot would land on a face. A one-word hook keeps the normal headline. Offer a daylight and a dusk version. Before filing a cover, crop it to the middle 4:5 and the middle 3:4 and look at both, beside the last cover posted. Then show it in the Instagram preview (section 5). Crop or choose photos so no client number plates, phone numbers or street addresses show.
- **Facebook page cover (the banner at the top of her page), an option on every campaign:** read the pack's Facebook page cover rules for the size, the safe box, what the words may say and the one small-capitals exception. Steps: ask on the decision page "Do you want a new Facebook page cover for this campaign?"; only when the job has a landscape photo (if there is none, don't ask; say so and that Laura would need to take one); suggest three wordings in the board's shape and let him pick; make two files from the campaign's best wide photo, with words and plain; check the result on a phone and a computer. File in `Facebook - Page cover (1640x624)`: `01 - Page cover - With words.jpg`, `02 - Page cover - Plain.jpg`. Laura changes the cover herself; never touch her page.

## 2. Video
- **Caption style:** read the pack's caption rules (the three styles and how they move). Pink box is the default reel; Wild font and Classic font are made only when asked. One version by default, not three. `tools/river-fox/captions/anim.cjs` draws all three from `brand-pack.json`.
- **Caption timing (build detail, not on the canvas):** each word fades in about 0.06s before she says it (fade about 0.13s). Find each word's onset from her voice at 20 ms steps, never from a transcript alone; each separate burst is a word. A held word's letters arrive one by one across the hold; never split a phrase across screens.
- Captions never cover her face: check every caption frame against where her face is.
- **Headline and closing text on video:** read the pack's rules for headline and closing text on video. Make them with `text/mkcopy2.cjs`, which prints where the lettering sits and will not write one outside the safe area. If the small capitals line under the closing text does not fit inside the safe area, remove it rather than shrink or move the headline. No casual or lowbrow wording.
- Text on video is narrower than a cover (the pack gives the limit; `mkcopy2.cjs` fits to it). Never reuse a cover's width on video.
- **Safe areas:** the pack has the numbers for Reels, Stories and covers; `mkcopy2.cjs` and `reels/check.py` read them from `brand-pack.json`. Use `STORY=1` / `--story` for a Story.
- **Audio** (the pack's sound rules): no music bed, not even looped room tone (it was heard as a repeating noise). Trim her speech to just around her words with a light gate.
- Timelapse: two storyboards, build first and hook first (section 0, step 4). Fix flicker and colour with `timelapse/stabilise.py`, crop centred on the arch with margins, no pan if the arch fills the width. The finale text only goes on the time-lapse if its last frame shows the finished job (`reel-E-timelapse-build.sh` shows that frame and waits for a yes); otherwise cut to Laura's own reveal and put the finale there.
- Cutting (build detail): hard cuts, never in the first 4 seconds, no animated zooms or shakes, no end card. End a talking shot with her eyes open, once her words have finished.
- **Run `reels/check.py` on every finished video, draft and final, and read its sheet before sending or filing:** frame 0, the frame either side of every cut, every text against the red safe lines, where sound starts and stops. A stray frame or text outside the lines fails. Say “inside” or “clean” only for what it measured.
- **The working folder is kept: `~/river-fox-work/<job>` on the Mac** (never a temporary folder, never Drive). The reel scripts save the picture with no text on it in its `plates/`. A re-cut is made from those, never from a finished video; if it has to be, cut by frame number and re-check, because it inherits that video's faults.
- Cut after an opening close-up as soon as it has finished (about 1.8s), not later.
- Wild card Story: an option, offered on talking clips where she moves (the pack's wild card Story; `tools/river-fox/cutout/`).
- Exports: drafts are 540x960 on purpose. Final 1080x1920, CRF 18, max 25 Mbps (about 105 MB for 35 s), one file per option. A 4K file only when asked, and say it is upscaled unless the footage is real 4K. No separate "master" copies.

## 3. Words
- **Voice: `docs/voice.md`** (in this repo) is the only place the voice rules are written: which voice goes on which channel (website and Google Ads calm, Google Business halfway, Instagram and Facebook in Laura's own playful voice), her seven voice rules, the banned words and the curly quote marks. Read it before writing any words; never copy its rules here. Read Laura's recent captions too (her Instagram in the Browser pane) and match how she writes.
- Caption layout (line breaks, where the call to action and hashtags go) is in the pack's caption and hashtag rules.
- **Hashtags** (the rule is in the pack's caption and hashtag rules; the tags are in the Hashtag bank sheet): read the sheet's Rules tab fresh for every post, never from memory or an older caption, and fill its five slots from the Hashtag bank tab (status Active only), add the town to the bank if it is new, never use anything on the Never use tab, and afterwards add one row per post to the Post log tab. "River Fox Events Surrey" is not a hashtag.
- **A caption file for a single post** (a `.txt` beside the video in the post's folder, `Caption - <post>.txt`): only the caption to copy, nothing else. An `INSTAGRAM` heading, the caption with its five hashtags, then a `FACEBOOK` heading and the same caption with "message me" and the page link. No file list, no posting steps, no cover how-to, no notes, no prompts for the Brand Pack chat (Matt, 24 Nov 2026). Watch the video before writing it, so the words match what happens.
- **Copy doc** (the last step of every job): a Google Doc `Copy doc - <job>` at the top of the job folder with only these five sections, in this order, each written in its channel's voice (`docs/voice.md`): Instagram Reel, Instagram post, Facebook post (each caption ends with its five hashtags after one blank line, laid out as the pack's caption rules show, no column of dots; Facebook says "message me" instead of "DM me" and adds the page link), Google Business post (halfway voice, no hashtags, then "Button: Book" and the page link), then Google Ads (calm voice, Book now button, page link, 3 headlines of 30 characters or fewer, 2 descriptions of 90 or fewer). Nothing else. The Worplesdon Copy doc (21 Nov 2026) is the model to copy. A thin grey divider line sits between the sections (a top border on each heading after the first, `update_doc` `updateParagraphStyle` `borderTop`).

## Review cards (Matt, 21 November 2026)
A client's review made into an image. This section is the whole rule for review cards; where it differs from the general steps (section 0's questions and decision pages, section 4's folders), this section wins.
- Make them with `tools/river-fox/review/review.cjs`: give it the size (`instagram` or `google`), the quote, the name and the output file. It takes every size, spot and colour from `brand-pack.json`, joins the last two words so no word sits alone, and prints each line's width and the space to the label, the name and every edge.
- Before each review job read the pack's review card rules (Instagram and Google Business). If the canvas can't be read, stop and tell Matt.
- The words: a short review is used whole, in the client's own words. A long one uses an excerpt as the pack describes it (whole sentences, word for word, the same excerpt on both cards); the tool refuses a long quote over the pack's word count rather than set it small. Say which sentences were left out when handing the cards over. Emojis are left off.
- **Spelling and grammar check every quotation before it goes on an image (Matt, 21 November 2026). Fix them even if anything older says typos stay as the client wrote them (Matt, same day).** Read the quote through and fix clear slips only: a misspelt or wrong word ("and beautiful" for "are beautiful"), a missing full stop or capital between sentences, a missing full stop at the end. Never reword, shorten, tidy her style or change her punctuation choices (her exclamation marks and "stunning" stay). Tell Matt every change made, old and new, when handing the cards over. The check includes a comma before "and" when it joins two full sentences ("are beautiful, and the natural colours suit..."). **Only this tool makes the review PNGs in Drive.** If a card in Drive came from anywhere else (the Claude chat re-exported Sarah's on 21 Nov and the full stops were lost), read it word by word and run this check before saying it is fine, then remake it here. This is for quotes designed onto images; the website shows reviews exactly as Google has them.
- Make both sizes every time (Instagram and Google Business). No caption.
- Check the result at phone size before filing: even lines, no single word on the last line, and a long quote must not have gone small.
- Filing: a review for a job that has its own folder goes in a `Review - <first name>` folder inside that job's folder, with older cuts in its `_Archive`. Otherwise file by channel in Social > Instagram > Review posts and Social > Google Business > Review posts.
- Come back with the finished cards filed and say where they are. Don't offer versions first.

## 4. Filing and names
- **Job and campaign folders: `NN - <location or event name>`** (e.g. `01 - Worplesdon garland and wreath`), 01 = newest; when adding a job, rename the others up by one (highest first). The type (Images or Video) is NOT in the name because one job holds both. **No Finder colour tags except one: the newest (01) gets the Purple Finder tag** (Finder has no pink; write it with `xattr -w -x com.apple.metadata:_kMDItemUserTags` and a binary plist of `Purple\\n3`, then `killall Finder`). When a new job becomes 01, remove the tag from the old one (`xattr -d`). It also has a pink Drive folder colour (`update_file` `folderColorRgb` `#f691b2`) for Drive on the web and phone; reset the old one to Drive's default grey (`#8f8f8f`). Archive folders are always `_Archive`.
- One folder per place it will be posted: `Instagram - Reels (9x16)`, `Instagram - Stories (9x16)`, `Instagram - Posts (3x4)`, `Facebook - Posts (4x5)` (with the same two covers first, made by `thumb.cjs` at the Facebook post size), `Google Business - Posts (4x3, no text)` (plain photos only, no covers, no text). Files are short and numbered and say the kind, not the place: `01 - Video - Pink box captions.mp4`, `05 - Cover - Dusk.jpg`, `03 - Photo - Front view.jpg`. Same photo names and order in every folder. Superseded files go to `_Archive`.
- Laura's originals stay where she uploaded them. Never overwrite; never leave scratch work in Drive.

Say what you checked.
